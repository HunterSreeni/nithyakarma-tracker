Panchangam Calendar

A proposal to replace the Referrals tab with a calendar that runs on the user's own tradition: the Tamil or Malayalam month is the calendar, and the Gregorian date is the footnote. Switch the phone below between traditions and between day, week and month. Every date, tithi, nakshatra, kalam time and observance shown is real data read from panchangam_days. Step back a month, or forward past the last loaded date, to see how missing panchangam data behaves.

REVISION 4One color for the whole calendar

Every view now sets its text in the dark saffron already used on the observance chips, --saffron-900. Day, week and month, the calendar numbers, the tithi and nakshatra names, the kalam times, the view switcher, the weekday heads, the legend and the observance list all read in the one color. The only exception is the gradient hero on the day view, where white is the only thing that works on that ground.

  • 9.37On a white card. The Panchangam rows, the kalam list, the week rows and the observance list.
  • 8.26On the paper screen. The weekday heads, the legend and the not-loaded cells.
  • 8.83On the saffron-50 tint. The kalam time chips, the observance chips and today's cell.
  • 7.66On the cream switcher. The day, week and month tabs.

With one text color, hierarchy comes from size and weight instead of tone, so the type scale had to carry it: the native script sits a full step above the Latin around it, and the secondary lines dropped to 500 weight rather than a lighter color. State that used to lean on color now leans on fill: the active view tab is a white pill, today is a ringed cell, and the disabled stepper is the one place opacity is used, since a disabled control is exempt from the contrast minimum.

YOUR CALL, APPLIEDNo completion dot

The green "all practices done" dot is gone from the month grid, and the practice ring is gone from the week rows. The calendar is a panchangam and nothing else. The grid now carries two marks only: gold for an observance, dark for a tharpanam day.

YOUR CALL, APPLIEDShow what is loaded, refuse the rest

Both behaviours, as asked. Step back to Aadi / Karkidakam: the days with rows render normally, the days without render as flat dashed cells, and a note under the grid says how many are missing. Step forward past Aavani / Chingam: the stepper is visibly disabled, and the same note explains why rather than leaving a dead button.

  • A missing row is never an error and never a spinner. It is an empty cell, the existing convention in usePanchangam.
  • The disabled stepper keeps its label and a real disabled style, so it reads as "nothing beyond here" rather than as a broken control.
  • Day view on a date with no row shows the same note in place of the panchangam cards. Week view shows a dashed row for each missing day.

WHAT CHANGESReferrals tab out, Calendar tab in

Referrals stops being a destination. The invite block already on the Profile page keeps the whole referral job: code, WhatsApp share, copy link, rewards copy. The freed bottom-nav slot goes to Calendar, which is the tab people open daily.

THE CORE IDEAThe month is the native month, not August

A Gregorian grid with Tamil labels sprinkled on it is not a Tamil calendar. So the month view runs Aavani 1 to 31 (17 Aug to 16 Sep 2026), the big number in each cell is the Tamil or Malayalam day, and the Gregorian date sits under it in small type. Stepping forward lands on Purattasi, not September.

  • The year label follows the tradition the way the Today box already does: Tamil gets the samvatsara name (பராபவ வருடம்), Malayalam gets the era count (കൊല്ലവർഷം 1202). These are different facts, not a translation pair.
  • Nakshatra and tithi use each tradition's own set, from the existing panchangamScript.js maps. Jyeshtha reads கேட்டை in Tamil and തൃക്കേട്ട in Malayalam.
  • One tradition at a time, chosen by the profile setting that already exists on the Profile page. No dual-column display.

THE THREE VIEWSDay, week, month

  • Day is the Today panchangam box with room to breathe: native date as the hero, tithi, nakshatra and varsham, all three kalams with native names and IST times, and any observance for that date.
  • Week is the planning view. Seven rows, native weekday and day number on the left, tithi and nakshatra in the middle, observance badge where there is one.
  • Month is the orientation view. Dense native-month grid, gold dot for an observance, dark dot for a tharpanam day. Under the grid, the month's observances as a dated list, which is what most people actually came for.

DATANothing new to build server-side

  • panchangam_days already holds every field the three views need. Today's box fetches one row by date, the calendar fetches a range instead.
  • Observances reuse the existing shared matcher in _shared/observanceMatch.ts, the same one ObservanceBanner already imports, so the calendar cannot drift from what the reminders fire on.
  • Fetching follows the ObservanceBanner pattern rather than the older raw-effect one: react-query with withDeadline and unwrap, so a hung request fails at the deadline instead of spinning forever, and a revisit paints from cache.
  • A native month spans two Gregorian months, so a month fetch is a date between range, not a month equality filter.
